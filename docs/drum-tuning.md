# 🥁 Tuning Your Drums: A Lesson

Welcome! Tuning is the fastest way to make any kit sound better, even a cheap
one. A budget kit that's tuned well beats an expensive kit that's tuned badly,
every time. It's a skill, not a talent. It feels slow the first few times, and
after a handful of tries it takes you ten minutes.

There are two parts. Read the one that matches your kit today:

- **[Part 1: Regular drum heads](#part-1-regular-drum-heads).** Your normal,
  full-volume kit. At the end I show you how to make it quiet with **RTOM Black
  Hole snap-on pads**.
- **[Part 2: Remo Silentstroke low-volume heads](#part-2-remo-silentstroke-low-volume-heads).**
  The top heads have been swapped for quiet mesh heads.

### What you need

- A **drum key**
- A **drumstick**
- A **towel or small pillow** (for the kick)
- A **quiet room**. Turn off the music and the TV. Your ears are the main tool.
- About **30 minutes** the first time. Don't rush it.

### Three words you'll see a lot

- **Batter head:** the one you hit (top of snare and toms, back of the kick).
- **Resonant head ("reso"):** the other one (bottom of snare and toms, front of
  the kick).
- **Tension rods / lugs:** the screws around the drum that you turn with the key.

# Part 1: Regular drum heads

## The five habits that make tuning work

Learn these once and use them on every drum, forever.

**1. Start from zero.** Loosen every rod on the head. Then turn each rod with
your *fingers only* until it just touches the hoop and stops. This is
**finger-tight**. Now every rod is starting from the same place. Skip this
step and nothing else will work.

**2. Tighten in a star, not a circle.** Go *across* the drum, like drawing a
star: tighten one rod, then the one opposite it, then move over one and do the
opposite again. Going around in a circle pulls the head crooked.

```
   6-lug drum          8-lug drum

      1                    1
   5     3              7     5
   4     6            4         3
      2                 6     8
                           2
```
*Follow the numbers 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 ... Each step jumps across the drum. Same
idea for a drum with more rods, like the kick.*

**3. Equal turns, every rod.** Every rod gets the *same* turn, every time
around. Start with **half turns**, then **quarter turns** as you get close,
then **tiny ⅛ turns** to finish. Counting turns is what keeps the head even.

**4. Seat a new head.** On a brand-new *batter* head, once it's finger-tight
and has had a couple of rounds of turns, press your palm firmly into the middle
a few times. A crackling sound is normal: it's the glue settling. Then check
all the rods again. Be much gentler with the thin *resonant* head: a light
press or none at all.

**5. One head at a time.** Mute the head you're *not* tuning so you only hear
the one you're working on. I show you how for each drum.

> 💡 **Teacher's tip:** New heads stretch. Check the tuning again after your
> first couple of practice sessions. That's normal, and it doesn't mean you
> did it wrong.

## The secret skill: making every rod match ("clearing" the head)

This is what makes a drum sound *clean* instead of wobbly. You'll use it on
every drum.

1. Rest one finger **lightly** in the **center** of the head. This mutes the
   main note so you can hear each spot clearly.
2. With the stick, tap gently about **1 inch (2–3 cm) in from each rod**, going
   around the drum.
3. Listen. Every spot should sound like **the same note**. Usually one or two
   stick out, a little higher or lower.
4. Fix only the ones that don't match, with **tiny ⅛ turns**. Low spot:
   tighten a little. High spot: loosen a little.
5. Go around once more. Changing one rod slightly nudges its neighbours.

✅ **You're done when** every spot sounds the same, and hitting the middle
gives one clear note that doesn't go "wah-wah".

## 1. The snare 

**What we're after:** a crisp, lively crack that responds even to your softest
ghost notes.

1. **Get ready.** Flip the throw-off lever to drop the snare wires off the
   bottom head. Set the drum on a chair or on your lap, which mutes the bottom
   head.
2. **Top head (batter).** Do habits 1–4, then tighten until it's
   **medium-high**. The stick should bounce back easily and the drum should
   say "pang", not "thud". Now clear the head (the secret skill).
3. **Bottom head (snare side).** Flip the drum over onto the stand or your
   lap, which mutes the top. This head is very thin, so be gentle. Tune it so
   it **sounds higher than the top**. That's what makes the wires crisp. But
   don't crank it: too tight and the wires choke.
4. **Wires.** Loosen the tension knob on the throw-off, flip the lever to put
   the wires back on, and turn the knob up **only until the wires sound
   tight and crisp**. Stop there. More tension than that just chokes the drum.

✅ **Test:** play soft taps near the edge. You should hear a crisp, clear
"tss" from the wires. Then play a hard backbeat: a big crack with no loose
rattle afterward.

## 2. The toms 

**What we're after:** a round, singing tone, with each tom clearly lower than
the one before it, like steps going down.

1. **Get ready.** Take the tom off its mount and lay it on carpet or a chair.
   The head touching the carpet is muted, so you tune the head facing up. Flip
   it to do the other head.
2. **Top head (batter).** Habits 1–4, then tighten until the stick **bounces
   nicely** and the tone is **round**. Too loose sounds flappy and papery. Too
   tight sounds like a boxy, pingy tin can. Clear the head.
3. **Bottom head (reso).** Pick one:
   - 🎵 **Same note as the top:** the tom rings long and even. A great place
     to start.
   - 🎸 **A little higher than the top:** shorter ring, and the note drops
     after you hit it ("doooown").

   Try both and trust your ears.
4. **Smallest tom first.** Tune your smallest tom first, then each bigger tom
   a bit lower. A good gap: hum the first two notes of **"Here Comes the
   Bride"**. That's roughly how far apart neighbouring toms should sound.

✅ **Test:** play a fill down the toms. It should sound like clear steps going
down. If two toms sound almost the same, spread them apart.

> 💡 **Teacher's tip:** If your snare buzzes whenever you hit a certain tom,
> that's the tom's note setting the wires off. Move that tom's bottom head up
> or down a little, and the buzz usually goes away.

## 3. The kick (bass drum)

**What we're after:** a deep, punchy "thud" you feel in your chest, and a
beater that bounces back so your foot doesn't get tired.

1. **Get ready.** Put a towel or small pillow inside the drum, touching the
   back head (the one you hit). Lightly touching the front head too is even
   better while you tune.
2. **Back head (batter). Use J.A.W. = "Just Above Wrinkles".** Habits 1–3,
   then press your palm into the middle of the head. You'll see little
   wrinkles near the rods. Tighten evenly, star pattern, **only until the
   wrinkles disappear**. Stop there. That's the deep, fat starting point.
   - Want more punch and bounce (for fast double kicks)? Go about **one more
     full turn** on every rod.
3. **Front head (reso).** Also just above wrinkles, or a little tighter. Press
   gently, if at all. A hole in the front head (a "port") is normal: it makes
   the boom shorter.

✅ **Test:** play single hits. You want a short, deep "doom", not a high
"boing" like a basketball. If it boings, loosen the back head toward J.A.W.

## When something sounds wrong

| What you hear | What to do first |
|---|---|
| The note wobbles, "wah-wah" | The rods aren't even. Clear the head again before you add any muffling |
| A tom rings forever | Tune its bottom head a little higher than the top. Only then try a gel pad (like Moongel) |
| A tom sounds dead, like cardboard | It's too loose, or the head is worn out. Tighten both heads evenly, or replace it |
| The snare sounds choked | Loosen the bottom head a little, or loosen the wire tension knob |
| The kick goes "boing" like a basketball | The back head is too tight. Loosen it toward J.A.W. |

> 💡 **Teacher's tip:** Gel and tape are for the *last 10%*. If the drum sounds
> bad, fix the tuning first. Muffling can't fix a head that isn't even.

## Option: going quiet with RTOM Black Hole snap-on pads

The Black Hole is a mesh pad on a rubber ring that **snaps on over your drum**.
You don't take anything off. Great for practising when others are sleeping.

1. **Tune the drum first**, using everything above. Then snap the pad on. When
   you take the pad off later, your drum is still in tune underneath.
2. **Snap it on.** Press the rubber ring down over the hoop all the way around
   until it grips. On heavy metal (die-cast) or wooden hoops it may not grip
   well. RTOM sells elastic hooks for that.
3. **Set the pad's feel.** Each pad has its own tension screw (a turnbuckle)
   under the rubber ring, and a small key comes with it. It changes the
   **bounce**, not the sound. Aim for about the same bounce as the real drum,
   so your practice carries over when you play without pads.
4. **Snare.** The wires still react through the pad. Leave them on for a bit
   of snare sound, or flip them off for near-silence.
5. **Kick.** There's a Black Hole for the kick too. Set it so the beater
   bounces back like it does on the real head.

# Part 2: Remo Silentstroke low-volume heads

Here your regular top heads have been **replaced** with Remo Silentstroke mesh
heads. They're very quiet, and they tune differently: **mesh doesn't really
have a note.** You're not tuning for sound anymore; you're tuning for
**feel**, meaning how the stick bounces.

## The five habits for mesh heads

**1. Same routine as before.** Start from zero (finger-tight), use the star
pattern, and give every rod equal turns. Everything from Part 1's habits still
applies.

**2. Go slowly.** Start with about **½ turn** on each rod past finger-tight.
From there, use only **⅛ turns**, or even smaller. Mesh goes from "great" to
"too tight" very quickly.

**3. Tune with your finger, not your ears.** Press a fingertip into the head
about 1 inch in from each rod. **Every spot should give the same amount.** That
is how you "clear" a mesh head. Then play a few strokes and feel the bounce.

**4. Pick your feel.**
- **Tighter:** more bounce and a little more tone. Great for fast singles,
  rolls, and working on speed.
- **Looser:** feels more like a relaxed acoustic kit, but you'll need to work
  a bit harder for your strokes to bounce back.

Either one is fine. Just make it *even*.

**5. Don't over-tighten.** If the drum starts to sound like a dead "thud" and
feels like hitting a board, you've gone too far. Loosen every rod the same
amount.

## Drum by drum

- **Bottom heads (if you still have regular ones there):** mesh moves much
  less air, so a loose bottom head hardly vibrates. Tune the bottom heads
  **higher than you normally would**. That gives the drum some body back.
- **Snare:** mesh on top, the regular thin snare head on the bottom, wires on.
  Keep the bottom head **tight** (like in Part 1) so the wires still react to
  your quieter hits. Set the wire tension knob until it sounds crisp.
- **Toms:** make each tom a little looser than the smaller one before it. Here
  that's more about the feel changing smoothly around the kit than about notes.
- **Kick:** tighten the mesh until the beater **bounces off cleanly** and
  doesn't sink in. There's no J.A.W. here, since mesh doesn't wrinkle like a
  regular head.

## When something feels wrong

| What you feel | What to do first |
|---|---|
| The stick sinks in, no bounce | Tighten every rod ⅛ turn, star pattern |
| A dead "thud", feels like a board | Too tight. Loosen every rod the same amount |
| One side feels different | The rods aren't even. Press 1 inch from each rod and even them out |
| The snare wires barely respond | Tighten the bottom snare head or the wire tension knob a little |

---

## Last word from your teacher

Don't expect perfection the first time. Tune, play for a few days, listen,
adjust. Every time you do it, your ears get sharper and it goes faster.
Pretty soon you'll hear a drum out of tune from across the room. Now go play! 🤘
