# 🥁 Drum Tuning Cheat Sheet

## The golden rules

1. **Start even.** Back every rod off, then spin each one down until it just
   touches the hoop (finger-tight). Everything below builds on this even start.
2. **Star pattern, equal turns.** Tighten across the drum (star/cross), never
   around the circle. Give every rod the *same* amount — half turns to get
   close, then quarter turns, then eighths.
3. **Seat new heads.** Batter (top): press firmly with your palm in the center
   a few times. You'll hear the glue crackle; that's normal. Then re-check
   every lug. Resonant (bottom): it's thinner, so press gently or not at all.
   Never punch it.
4. **Tune *up* to the note.** If a lug is too high, loosen it *below* the target
   and bring it back up. A rod that was loosened into place tends to drift.
5. **One head at a time.** Mute the head you're not tuning (see each drum
   below).
6. **New heads stretch.** Re-check after the first couple of sessions.

## 1. Snare

- **Setup:** Throw off the snare wires. Put the drum on a chair or your lap to
  mute the bottom while you tune the top. Flip it to do the bottom.
- **Batter:** Medium-high tension. Clear it (see *Fine-tuning* below) so every
  lug sounds the same about 1″ from the rim.
- **Resonant (snare side):** Noticeably tighter than the batter. That makes the
  wires crisp and sensitive. Too tight chokes the wires and you lose ghost-note
  response; too loose sounds flabby and rattly.
- **Wires:** Loosen the strainer knob, engage the throw-off, then tighten only
  until the wires stop buzzing loosely. Past that point, more tension just
  chokes the drum.

## 2. Toms

- **Setup:** Rest the tom on carpet or a chair to mute the bottom while you do
  the top, then flip it.
- **Batter:** Start where the stick bounces nicely and the tone is round, not
  papery (too loose) or boxy/pingy (too tight).
- **Resonant:** This sets the *character*:
  - **Same pitch as the batter:** longest sustain, steady note.
  - **Higher than the batter** (about a minor third is a common starting
    point): the note bends *down* after the hit. That's the classic "doooown"
    rock tom.
  - **Lower than the batter:** shorter, punchier, and the pitch can bend up.
  - Sources disagree on these relationships, so try each one and trust your
    ears.
- **Order:** Smallest tom first, then each bigger tom lower. Spacing them
  roughly a **third or a fourth** apart keeps them distinct. Hum a chord
  (e.g. the first notes of *Here Comes the Bride* go up a fourth) if that
  helps.
- **Snare buzz:** If the snare wires buzz when you hit a tom, move that tom's
  pitch slightly (usually the reso) until the buzz goes away.

## 3. Kick

- **Setup:** A pillow or folded towel inside the drum, touching the batter,
  and ideally lightly touching the front head too while you tune.
- **Batter (back):** J.A.W., *Just Above Wrinkles*. Press the center with your
  palm and tighten evenly *only* until the wrinkles disappear. That gives low
  thud and a relaxed feel. A turn or so more gives more punch and rebound
  (faster double strokes).
- **Resonant (front):** Slightly tighter than the batter, around 1–1½ turns
  past finger-tight, to push the note out front. Press gently at most. If it
  has a port hole, it will sustain less and mic up more easily.

## Fine-tuning ("clearing") a head

1. Mute the other head, then rest a finger lightly in the **center** of the head
   you're tuning.
2. Tap gently with a stick about **1″ in from each rod**, going around the drum.
3. Find the odd one out, and move it in tiny **⅛ turns** until every lug
   matches. Tune *up* to the note (rule 4).
4. Re-check the first lug last. Changing one rod slightly affects its
   neighbours.

## Quick fixes

| Problem | Try first |
|---|---|
| Wobbling, "wah-wah" pitch | The lugs aren't even. Clear the head again before adding any muffling |
| Tom rings forever | Lower the reso relative to the batter, *then* consider a gel pad |
| Dead, cardboard tom | Too loose, or the head is worn out. Tighten both heads evenly, or replace |
| Snare sounds choked | Loosen the snare-side head a little, or back off the strainer |
| Kick sounds "basketball" | Batter is too tight. Back it down toward J.A.W. |
